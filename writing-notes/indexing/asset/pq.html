Step 0: The Raw Vectors

In a real vector database, vectors can have 768 or 1536 dimensions. Storing millions of these as raw 32-bit floats takes massive amounts of RAM.

Here, our toy dataset has 5 vectors, each with 8 dimensions. Our goal is to compress them so we can search them faster and cheaper.

Step 1: Subspace Splitting

The "Product" in Product Quantization means we split the large vector space into the Cartesian product of smaller sub-spaces.

We split our 8-dimensional vectors into 4 subspaces (m=4). Now, instead of one big 8D vector, we treat it as four tiny 2D vectors. Look at the database on the left—notice the gaps!

Step 2: Training Codebooks (K-Means)

For each of the 4 subspaces, we look at all the 2D chunks and run K-Means clustering. We find k=4 centroids per subspace. These 4 centroids make up our "Codebook" for that subspace.

Step 3: Quantization (Compression)

Now we compress! For every 2D chunk in our database, we find its nearest centroid in the codebook and replace the floating-point numbers with the Integer ID (0, 1, 2, or 3) of that centroid.

Look at the database on the left. The 8 floats are gone, replaced by just 4 tiny integers! We have massively reduced memory usage.

Step 4: Asymmetric Distance Computation (ADC)

How do we search compressed vectors without decompressing them? When the Query Vector arrives, we slice it into 4 subspaces too. We compute the distance from the query's sub-vectors to all centroids and build a Lookup Table.

To score a database vector, we simply look up its IDs in this table and sum them up! No math needed for the DB vectors.
